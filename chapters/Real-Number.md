# Introduction

This document explains the **elementary properties of the field of real
numbers**, denoted by ℝ. It assumes you are familiar with basic
mathematical concepts related to real numbers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. What Are Real Numbers?

Real numbers ℝ include: - All integers: …, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, … - All
rational numbers: numbers expressible as fractions, e.g.,
$\frac{3}{4}, -5, 0.25$ - All irrational numbers: numbers **not**
expressible as fractions, e.g., $\sqrt{2}, \pi, e$

Thus, the real numbers cover the entire continuous number line.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Ordering and Inequalities

Real numbers have a **natural order**: - For any *a*, *b* ∈ ℝ, exactly
one is true: *a* &lt; *b*, *a* = *b*, or *a* &gt; *b*.

**Inequality properties:** - Adding or subtracting the same number
preserves inequality. - Multiplying or dividing by a positive number
preserves inequality. - Multiplying or dividing by a negative number
**reverses** inequality.

**Example:** - 3 &lt; 5 → 3 + 2 &lt; 5 + 2 ⟹ 5 &lt; 7 - Multiply by −1:
3 &lt; 5 ⟹ −3 &gt; −5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Completeness Property

The real numbers satisfy the **least upper bound (supremum)
property**: - Every nonempty set bounded above has a least upper bound
in ℝ.

This is **not true** for rational numbers alone.

**Example:** - The set {*x* ∈ ℝ : *x*<sup>2</sup> &lt; 2} has supremum
$\sqrt{2}$, even though $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Algebraic and Geometric Intuition

You should: - Manipulate algebraic expressions confidently. - Visualize
real numbers as points on a continuous number line, understanding
intervals and distances.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Additional Notes

-   **Density:** Between any two real numbers, there exists a rational
    and an irrational number.
-   **Absolute value:** |*x*| gives the distance from zero on the number
    line.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Summary

By knowing the properties of ℝ, you can engage confidently with higher
mathematics such as analysis, calculus, and algebra.
